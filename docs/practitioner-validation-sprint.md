# Practitioner Validation Sprint

Internal operating brief. Use real Chicago projects, but keep participant names, exact addresses, financial documents, and sensitive business information out of the scorecard.

## Decision

Determine whether Chicago Incentive Explorer helps a practitioner and project owner move from an address to a clear, appropriately framed next step and a useful local-support option.

This is a five-session learning sprint. It is not an incentive-application cohort, financing screen, eligibility review, or endorsement of any organization. A successful sprint can expand into the existing 12-person cohort and then a separately approved 90-day assisted-navigation pilot.

Each participant should use a real project address and the closest project archetype. Do not invent project facts to force a result.

## Participants

Recruit one person for each case. Include at least:

- Two business owners or operators.
- One property owner or developer.
- One participant referred by a financing or business-support organization.
- One public, chamber, corridor, or community-development practitioner who can assess role clarity and handoff language.

The practitioner may observe a project-owner session or test an anonymized recent case. The Explorer should be framed as complementing trusted local organizations, not replacing them.

## Thirty-Minute Session

### 1. Context - 5 minutes

Ask only:

- What is the project trying to accomplish?
- What address is being considered?
- What decision or conversation is coming next?
- What information does the participant already have?

Do not collect documents or ask the participant to disclose confidential financial information.

### 2. Unguided task - 10 minutes

Give the participant the case link and say:

> Use this as you would on your own. Please talk through what you think the report is telling you and what you would do next.

Do not explain program rules, interpret organization fit, or rescue the participant unless they cannot proceed.

### 3. Local-support task - 5 minutes

Ask:

> Find an organization or support path you would consider contacting. What do you think that organization can help with, and what would you prepare before contacting it?

If the participant records an introduction or materials-review request, confirm that it is a request awaiting review and routing. Do not say anything was sent, accepted, scheduled, or approved unless a verified system or organization response confirms it.

### 4. Debrief - 7 minutes

Ask:

1. What useful next step did the report give you?
2. What felt unclear, cramped, too directive, or incomplete?
3. Did any wording sound like eligibility, approval, guaranteed funding, or endorsement?
4. Was the local-support option relevant to this project? Why or why not?
5. What would you need before taking the next step?

### 5. Diagnostic - 3 minutes

Complete `docs/practitioner-validation-scorecard.csv`. Use categorical observations, not a participant score.

## Outcome States

- `Clear`: participant completed the task and explained the next step without correction.
- `Partial`: participant found useful information but needed clarification or facilitator help.
- `Blocked`: participant could not complete the task or the product created a material misunderstanding.
- `Not observed`: the session did not reach this part of the workflow.

Request status must use one of:

- `Not requested`
- `Recorded - introduction`
- `Recorded - 1:1 review`
- `Acknowledged by organization`
- `Connected`

Never infer acknowledgment or connection from a click or a recorded request.

## Safety Flags

Record a safety flag if the participant believes any of the following after reading the report:

- The project is eligible or approved.
- A displayed amount is a promised award or project budget.
- A listed organization endorsed the project or is required to assist.
- A recorded introduction or 1:1 request has already been sent, accepted, or scheduled.
- A permit filing proves construction completion, a vacancy lead proves availability, or a historical investment is current funding.

Pause expansion and correct the language if any safety misunderstanding persists after the participant rereads the relevant section.

## Sprint Decision

Expand to the 12-person cohort only when:

- At least four of five participants generate a report without facilitator rescue.
- At least four of five can state a concrete next step in their own words.
- At least three of five take or intentionally prepare a useful action.
- Every incorrect or irrelevant support match is reviewed.
- No participant leaves with a material eligibility, dollar, endorsement, or handoff-status misconception.

Rethink the affected step when two or more sessions show the same friction. Treat one-off preferences as observations, not automatic roadmap commitments.

## What Analytics Mean

The private analytics dashboard groups these five campaign links into a pilot funnel:

- Starts
- Searches
- Distinct reports generated
- Distinct reports where local support was viewed
- Distinct reports with a support action
- Introduction or 1:1 review requests recorded

These are product-usage signals. They do not prove an application, acknowledgment, connection, approval, financing, incentive award, or dollars deployed. Qualitative session notes remain the primary evidence at this sample size.

## Closeout

Within one business day of the fifth session:

1. Review the funnel and scorecard together.
2. Group repeated friction by report comprehension, program context, preparation, local support, or handoff status.
3. Fix safety or factual issues before growth work.
4. Choose one workflow improvement supported by at least two sessions.
5. Record an explicit expand, revise-and-retest, or stop decision.
